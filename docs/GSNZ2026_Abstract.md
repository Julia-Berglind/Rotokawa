# Characterising the Brittle-Ductile Transition Beneath the Rotokawa Geothermal Field Using Joint Seismic and Magnetotelluric Interpretation

**Julia Berglind**<sup>1</sup>, **Finnigan Illsley-Kemp**<sup>1</sup>, **Alison Kirkby**<sup>2</sup>, **Isabelle Chambefort**<sup>2</sup>, **Saeid Jalilinasrabady**<sup>2</sup>

<sup>1</sup> School of Geography, Environment and Earth Sciences, Victoria University of Wellington  
<sup>2</sup> Earth Sciences New Zealand

---

Superhot geothermal resources, hosted near or below the brittle-ductile transition (BDT), can provide up to three times higher production enthalpy than conventional geothermal fluids. New Zealand's supercritical resource base could support up to 2000 MW of generation capacity, potentially meeting up to 35% of national electricity demand by 2050. This positions superhot geothermal as a major contributor to New Zealand's electricity supply, offering continuous, dispatchable baseload power as demand grows. This potential is contingent on resolving the BDT's structure and physical properties, since its depth and geometry are hypothesised to exert primary control on where superhot fluids can be accessed. The Rotokawa geothermal field is a strong target for superhot geothermal exploration due to its high regional heat flow, but the structure of the BDT below it remains poorly constrained, with considerable uncertainty in its depth and how it varies across the field.

This project aims to characterise the natural behaviour of the BDT beneath Rotokawa to inform superhot geothermal exploration. A new seismic node array will be deployed across the field, and the resulting dataset will be processed alongside existing magnetotelluric data to characterise the structure and properties of the BDT. Jointly interpreting these two independent datasets will allow us to resolve BDT depth and geometry with greater confidence.

The resulting characterisation will directly support New Zealand's DeepHeat Programme's assessment of superhot geothermal potential within the country and improve confidence in future exploration.
